A Son's Tribute

The Hands That Built Countless Homes

"When you build a dining table, you aren't just joining pieces of wood. You are creating the place where a family will sit together, laugh, and share meals for thirty years."

Growing up, the smell of fresh wood shavings, beeswax polish, and teak sawdust filled our home. For as long as I can remember, my father woke up before sunrise, packed his worn canvas bag with hand planes, chisels, and a measuring tape, and headed out.

In his early years, he didn't own a pickup truck or fancy power tools. He cycled through dirt trails to reach distant farming villages whenever someone’s roof rafters creaked or when a young couple needed their first wooden bed. He never complained about the heat, the dust, or carrying heavy timber planks.

Over the last 15 years, our region has seen rapid modernization. Fancy interior design studios opened in the big cities with glass storefronts, charging exorbitant commissions while sub-contracting cheap materials. But my father never changed his philosophy:

  • ✔ Direct Honest Pricing: He charges fair labor rates and tells the customer the exact timber cost without inflating prices.
  • ✔ Zero Compromise on Quality: He refuses to use hollow boards or counterfeit plywood that falls apart when water touches it.
  • ✔ Lifetime Accountability: If an old grandmother in a village calls saying a door hinge feels tight after 5 years, he still stops by to oil and adjust it with a smile.

Today, with modern precision tools and calibrated modular techniques, he builds high-end acrylic modular kitchens, luxury wardrobes, and carved doors that rival any metropolitan showroom. I built this website for him so more families who appreciate true craftsmanship can discover his work.
